Self-healing servers with Event-Driven Ansible and Ascender

A crashed service used to mean a night call and 20 to 30 minutes of downtime. I built a guarded self-healing loop that brings it back in about half a minute, with no human involved, and pages on-call only when automation should stop.

S

Situation

A service crashes in the middle of the night. Monitoring sends an alert, someone wakes up, connects to the VPN, logs in to the server, reads the logs and, in most cases, restarts it. That is 20 to 30 minutes of downtime for a task a machine can do in seconds.

The usual shortcut, systemd’s Restart=always, restarts blindly: no configuration check, no health verification, no audit trail, no limit across incidents and no escalation. For a team that wants to know who changed what, and why, an unaudited fix is not an option.

T

Task

Let a server repair a known failure on its own, and still stay under control:

  • Every automatic action is a normal Ascender job, with the same credential, log and access control as a run started by a person.
  • Least privilege: the component that reacts to alerts can launch one job template and nothing else.
  • Never trust the event, and stop after repeated failures instead of hiding a real problem.
  • Page a human, with the evidence already collected, when automation cannot or should not fix it.
  • Everything as code, so the whole chain can be reviewed and rebuilt.
A

Action

Prometheus detects, Alertmanager routes, ansible-rulebook decides and Ascender acts. Each piece does one job:

  1. Detectnode_exporter exports the state of allow-listed systemd units only. Prometheus scrapes it every 5 seconds, and SelfHealServiceDown fires after 10 seconds, marked as an alert automation may try to fix.
  2. RouteAlertmanager only routes: self-healing alerts go to Event-Driven Ansible, everything else goes straight to on-call.
  3. Decideansible-rulebook launches one Ascender job template, limited to the affected host. It holds no SSH key and no inventory, and its account, svc_eda, may execute that one template only.
  4. Remediate with guardrailsThe playbook validates the request (one host, a valid unit on that host’s allow-list), records evidence, applies a circuit breaker of 3 restarts in 10 minutes, runs nginx -t before restarting, then checks that the unit is active and /healthz answers HTTP 200.
  5. Escalate independentlyA second Prometheus rule pages on-call if the service is still down after 2 minutes. It needs nothing from the automation, so it also covers ansible-rulebook or Ascender being down.
  6. Prove itA Chaos test job template breaks the service on purpose. Roles, rulebook, alert rules and the Ascender objects (templates, account, permissions and token) are all code, managed with the awx.awx collection.

What testing changed

  • The playbook took 16 s, but syncing the inventory on every launch added 45 s more. A dedicated inventory, synced on each push, cut the job start to under a second.
  • The circuit breaker passed every review and failed its first chaos test: a literal \n meant the restart history never reached the limit. Only testing the guardrail found it.
  • Escalating with Alertmanager’s group_wait paged a flapping service that healed by itself and missed the one that opened the breaker. A Prometheus timer for each incident fixed both.
R

Result

Every scenario was triggered with the Chaos test job template:

  • Process crash (SIGKILL)8 of 8 healed automatically, with 31–39 s of total outage (detection 15–19 s, recovery 16–21 s).
  • Manual stopHealed, with 35 s of total outage.
  • 4 crashes in 10 minutesThe first 3 were healed. The 4th opened the circuit breaker, and on-call was paged 2 minutes later.
  • Broken nginx configurationNo restart: escalated in 14 s with the exact nginx -t error.

What it means for a team

  • Lower MTTR: about half a minute instead of 20 to 30 minutes of manual work for a crashed process.
  • Fewer night pages: on-call is woken only when automation has stopped, with the evidence already in the job log.
  • Audit trail: each fix is an Ascender job that shows the alert that triggered it, the commit that ran and what changed.
  • Knowledge as code: the runbook is a reviewed playbook in Git, not something only a senior engineer remembers.

From lab to production

  • Run the rulebook as an EDA Controller activation instead of a systemd service.
  • Run Prometheus and Alertmanager in high availability, with a real paging service for on-call.
  • Serve Ascender over HTTPS and keep the token in a secrets manager.
  • Start with frequent, low-risk incidents (crashed services, full disks, expired certificates) and add one remediation at a time, each with its own guardrails and chaos test.
